## 2. The single highest-value question: what did the 2026 monsoon actually do?

This falls squarely inside the quarter, so I treated it as the priority.

**Answer: a very bad June, a good July, and a quarter that ended roughly 12% below
normal cumulative rainfall — "below normal", but not a drought.** And, critically,
**Indian tractor demand ignored it.**

### 2.1 What happened, month by month

| Period | Rainfall vs LPA | Note |
|---|---|---|
| IMD updated seasonal forecast (issued late May 2026) | **90% of LPA** | "Below normal" for Jun–Sep |
| June 2026 | **~60% of LPA (−40%)** | Driest June in over a decade; reported as 5th-lowest June (99.5 mm) since 1901 |
| 9 July 2026 (cumulative) | 205 mm vs 233.1 mm LPA = **−14%** | Strong first-week recovery |
| 18 July 2026 (cumulative) | **−24%** | Ten-day monsoon break re-widened the gap |
| July 2026 (month) | 290.3 mm vs 280.5 mm normal = **+4%** | 5th consecutive above-normal July |
| **2 August 2026 (cumulative)** | **−12%** | **This is the Deere Q3 quarter-end date** |
| 9 August 2026 (cumulative) | −11% | Continued slow narrowing |
| Aug–Sep 2026 outlook | **below normal (<94% LPA)** | IMD; El Niño strengthening |

Kharif sowing tracked the rain and then largely caught up: area was **−23% YoY at
end-June**, narrowing sharply to **~−3% YoY at end-July**, though total planted area
was still trailing by **3.88 m ha** in late July. The catch-up was uneven — pulses at
77.0% of normal area (vs 82.2% a year earlier) and coarse cereals 86.4% (vs 93.4%)
both remained well behind.

### 2.2 Why this matters less to Q3 than you would expect

The classic monsoon → tractor transmission runs with a lag of one to two quarters:
rain drives kharif output, output drives the October–December harvest income, income
drives festive-season and post-harvest tractor buying. A deficient June therefore
lands mostly on **Deere Q4 FY2026 and FY2027**, not on Q3 FY2026 shipments.

More importantly, the demand data for the exact Q3 window flatly contradicts the
rainfall data. See below.

### 2.3 The Aug–Sep tail is the real risk, and it is outside this quarter

IMD's below-normal Aug–Sep call with a strengthening El Niño is the thing to carry
forward. If it verifies, the FY2026 Q4 and FY2027 Indian setup deteriorates
materially — and note the guidance timeline in §5: management's "roughly flat Asia"
was set on **21 May 2026**, *before* the June failure was known.

## 3. India — the dominant country in this region

### 3.1 Tractor retails in the exact Q3 window (FADA registrations)

| Month | Industry 2025 | Industry 2026 | Ind. YoY | John Deere 2025 | John Deere 2026 | JD YoY | JD share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| May | 74,744 | 83,092 | +11.2% | 5,900 | 6,039 | +2.4% | 7.89% → 7.27% |
| June | 80,456 | 100,818 | +25.3% | 6,178 | 7,165 | +15.9% | 7.68% → 7.11% |
| July | 91,604 | 117,349 | +28.1% | 6,986 | 8,087 | +15.7% | 7.63% → 6.89% |
| **May–Jul** | **246,804** | **301,259** | **+22.1%** | **19,064** | **21,291** | **+11.7%** | **7.72% → 7.07%** |

Two findings, and the second is the one the team should carry:

1. **Indian tractor demand accelerated through the quarter** — +11%, +25%, +28% —
   in the teeth of the worst June rainfall in a decade. Domestic wholesales
   (factory dispatches) rose 11.9% in June to 126,112 units, confirming this is
   real shipment volume and not just registration timing.
2. **Deere is participating but losing share.** JD India grew +11.7% against an
   industry up +22.1%, and share slipped ~0.65pp to 7.07%. Deere skews to higher
   horsepower; the current surge is broader-based and affordability-led. Do not
   model Deere India on the industry growth rate — use roughly half of it.

### 3.2 Why demand held up despite the rain

- **GST cut from 12% to 5% on tractors, effective 22 September 2025.** Cut sticker
  prices by ₹40,000–₹100,000 depending on horsepower. This is the dominant driver
  and it also depresses the year-ago base: buyers deferred purchases in mid-2025
  ahead of the reform, so May–July 2025 is an artificially weak comparative. A
  meaningful slice of the +22% is base effect, not underlying acceleration.
- **Record Indian FY2026 (Apr 2025–Mar 2026)**: ICRA estimates a record
  11.3–11.5 lakh units, wholesales +22.8% in the first 11 months. FADA noted
  tractor retails crossed one million units for the first time ever.
- **MSP support**: kharif 2026-27 MSPs approved 13 May 2026 — inside the quarter —
  with common paddy up ₹72 (+3.0%) to ₹2,441/quintal, and steeper percentage rises
  for pulses, oilseeds and coarse cereals.
- **TREM V deferral.** The April 2026 emission-norm transition had been generating
  pre-buy demand; it was deferred (a February 2026 proposal pushes final TREM V
  implementation to 2032). This removed a cost cliff — supportive for affordability,
  but it also **removes a pre-buy tailwind** that had been flattering volumes. ICRA
  expects Indian FY2027 (Apr 2026–Mar 2027) growth to slow to just **1–4%** on the
  elevated base. That deceleration is the forward risk.

### 3.3 Pune, exports and tariffs

Deere's **Pune (Sanaswadi) Works is a SAT factory** (per the FY2025 10-K property
table) with capacity around 72,000 tractors, and it exports fully-built tractors to
the US, Japan, Germany and Europe. Two relevant developments:

- On the Q3 FY2025 call, management named **India** as one of four drivers (with
  Europe, steel and Japan) of roughly two-thirds of the then-$600m tariff impact.
- By the **Q1 FY2026 call (19 Feb 2026)** management cited "some relief in India"
  as partially offsetting the ~$1.2bn FY2026 tariff estimate. Externally, the
  India–US interim trade agreement cut the 50% reciprocal tariff on Indian exports
  to **18%**. This is a margin and cost story more than a revenue story for this
  desk, but it de-risks the Pune export channel.
- Structural: at Investor Day (8 Dec 2025) management sized the Indian tractor
  industry at **900,000+ units** with Deere at **just under 9% share**, and said
  that from later in 2026 **every new self-propelled machine sold in India will be
  connectivity-enabled**, with 10,000+ Indian contractors already on Operations
  Center. Long-duration, not a Q3 driver.

### 3.4 FX is the offset that kills the unit growth

USD/INR averaged **95.46 in Q3 FY2026 vs 85.64 in Q3 FY2025** — the rupee
depreciated 11.5%, a **−10.3% translation drag** on Indian revenue in USD. So
+11.7% Deere India retail units becomes roughly flat-to-modestly-up in reported
dollars, before mix and before the wholesale-vs-retail wedge.

## 4. The rest of the region

### 4.1 Australia — deteriorating crop, appreciating currency

ABARES' **June 2026 Australian Crop Report** (published inside the quarter) forecasts
2026-27 winter crop production **down 21% to 54.5 Mt**, with wheat **down 26% to
26.7 Mt** (23% below the five-year average) on area **down 12% to 10.9 m ha**, the
smallest wheat area since 2019-20. The Bureau of Meteorology put a **60–80% chance
of below-average June–August rainfall** across the cropping regions of Queensland,
NSW, Victoria, South Australia and Western Australia.

Read-through: Australia is the main **PPA** driver in this region and the crop
outlook turned negative *during* the quarter. But two things cushion Q3
specifically — the region is coming off a large 2025-26 crop (implied ~69 Mt), so
farm incomes funding current-quarter deliveries are still healthy; and the AUD
appreciated, averaging **0.7055 USD/AUD vs 0.6490** a year earlier, a **+8.7%
translation tailwind**. The crop downgrade bites order intake for Deere Q1 FY2027,
not Q3 FY2026 revenue.

### 4.2 China and construction — a genuine upturn

Chinese excavator sales rose **26.4% YoY in H1 2026** to 152,320 units
(domestic +20.4% to 79,025; exports +33.5% to 73,295), and CCMA reported **+25% YoY
in July 2026**, sustaining the uptrend. CNY also strengthened (6.78 vs 7.19, a
+6.1% translation tailwind). Deere's direct Chinese CF share is small, but the
cycle direction is unambiguously supportive and Wirtgen has a Langfang operation.

### 4.3 Middle East and Africa — roadbuilding is the story

Deere's FY2026 outlook has **global roadbuilding up ~10%**, the strongest line in
the whole industry outlook, and this region is where a disproportionate share of
that lands. The GCC infrastructure pipeline is roughly $2 trillion with Saudi
Vision 2030 megaprojects (NEOM, Qiddiya, Red Sea) plus post-World-Cup legacy work
driving earthmoving and road machinery. This is the mechanism behind CF revenue in
this region growing +28.6% in Q1 and +33.2% in Q2 FY2026. Caveat: the sizing
figures here come from commercial market-research summaries, not official
statistics — I have flagged them as lower-grade evidence and have **not** used them
to set the forecast, only to explain direction.

### 4.4 Japan and Southeast Asia

JPY averaged **160.54 vs 145.18**, a **−9.6% translation drag**. Japan was named by
management as one of the four main tariff-cost drivers on the Q3 FY2025 call. I
found no quarter-specific Southeast Asia disclosure in the corpus; this is a
genuine gap rather than a zero.

## 5. Management's own read, and how it moved

The most useful single signal in the corpus is that Deere **upgraded Asia three
times in six months**:

| Date | Guidance for Asia ag industry sales | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| 26 Nov 2025 (Q4 FY2025 call) | **down ~5%** | "down 5% following slight gains in India last year" |
| 19 Feb 2026 (Q1 FY2026 call) | **flat to down 5%** | "The Indian market is now expected to only be down slightly from the strong level seen in 2025" |
| 21 May 2026 (Q2 FY2026 call) | **roughly flat** | "roughly flat year-over-year, mainly driven by modest improvements within the India market" |

The direction of revision is consistently positive, and the FADA data since then
suggests the 21 May view was, if anything, still too cautious on volumes.

**But note the date.** The 21 May guidance predates the June rainfall failure. When
management updates on 20 August they will be doing so with a −12% cumulative
monsoon and a below-normal Aug–Sep forecast in hand. My expectation is that
**revenue lands fine but the forward Asia commentary gets more cautious** — that is
the asymmetry to watch on the call.

## 6. How I built the estimate

### 6.1 The history (30 quarters, FY2019 Q1 – FY2026 Q2)

Extracted by script from the ASC 606 footnote in every 10-Q and 10-K in the corpus.
Deere never publishes a standalone Q4, so **Q4 of each year is derived** as
fiscal-year total minus the nine-month figure from that year's Q3 10-Q. Every
derived quarter reconciles: segments sum exactly to the geography total in all 30
quarters (verified programmatically, zero mismatches).

FY2019 quarters predate the FY2020 re-segmentation and are only available on the
legacy Agriculture & Turf / Construction & Forestry basis; they are tagged
`AT_legacy` / `CF_legacy` in the CSV. The FY2019 full year was later restated onto
PPA/SAT/CF (PPA 1,039 / SAT 1,449 / CF 1,265 / FS 126), but **no quarterly
restatement for FY2019 was ever published** — that is a blank in the data, not a
zero. So the usable clean quarterly series on today's segment basis is **26 quarters,
FY2020 Q1 onward**, not 30.

### 6.2 Seasonality (the anchor)

Q3/Q2 sequential ratios for this region, FY2020–FY2025 (n=6):

| | PPA | SAT | CF | FS | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median (n=6) | 1.163 | 0.993 | 1.019 | 1.023 | **1.030** |
| Median ex-FY2020 (n=5) | 1.154 | 0.965 | 0.931 | 1.000 | 0.998 |
| Range | 0.845–1.412 | 0.867–1.100 | 0.784–1.267 | 0.963–1.067 | 0.922–1.239 |

**n=6 is far too small to treat as a distribution.** PPA in particular ranges from
0.845 to 1.412 — a 67% spread — which is why PPA carries a low-confidence flag. I
used seasonality as a sanity band, not as the estimator.

### 6.3 Segment reasoning

- **PPA 355 (+6.9%, seq 1.079).** Australia-led and the most volatile line here.
  Q2 came in at just +5.4% YoY; the ABARES downgrade landed during the quarter but
  hits ordering, not deliveries; AUD +8.7% is a real translation tailwind. I placed
  it below the seasonal median ratio (1.154) because global large ag is the weak
  spot and Deere guides PPA down 5–10% for the year. **Widest error bars of the four.**
- **SAT 440 (+12.0%, seq 0.987).** Best-evidenced line: I have Deere's own Indian
  retail units for all three months of the exact window (+11.7%). Against that,
  INR translation is −10.3% and Deere is ceding share. Local-currency growth looks
  strong (roughly +20%+); USD growth much less so. Positioned slightly below the
  Q2 level in sequential terms, consistent with the ex-2020 seasonal median of 0.965.
- **CF 380 (+21.4%, seq 1.030).** Momentum is +28.6% then +33.2% in Q1/Q2, driven by
  roadbuilding into the Middle East and a Chinese construction upturn. I deliberately
  forecast **deceleration** because Deere's own FY2026 CF guide (+20%) against H1
  actual (+31%) implies roughly +9–10% in H2 company-wide. Splitting the difference
  between momentum and the implied guide.
- **FS 55 (+3.8%, seq 1.019).** This line has printed between 52 and 56 for eight
  consecutive quarters. Highest-confidence number in the table, and immaterial to
  the aggregate either way.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| INR per USD | 85.64 | 95.46 | **−10.3%** |

precision here and a reason I did not attempt a bottom-up FX bridge.

## 7. Risks to the call

1. **Wholesale-vs-retail wedge (both directions).** I observe Indian *retail*
   registrations; Deere books *wholesale* shipments to dealers. Dealers typically
   build stock ahead of the Sept–Oct festive season, which would push Q3 shipments
   above retail. Equally, a cautious dealer reading the June rainfall could destock.
   I cannot see Indian dealer inventory.
2. **Monsoon Aug–Sep verification.** Below-normal with El Niño strengthening. Lands
   on Q4 FY2026/FY2027, but could cause management to guide Asia down on 20 August
   even if Q3 revenue is fine.
3. **Indian base effect reverses.** A large share of the +22% industry growth is the
   depressed pre-GST-cut 2025 comparative. From roughly October 2026 the comp turns
   hostile, and ICRA already models Indian FY2027 growth at just 1–4%.
4. **Deere India share loss continues.** Down ~0.65pp YoY and worsening each month
   (−0.62, −0.57, −0.74). If this accelerates, SAT misses low.
5. **CF order lumpiness.** Middle East roadbuilding revenue is project-driven; a
   single large delivery slipping across the quarter boundary moves CF several
   percent.
6. **Australia deteriorates faster than ABARES' June view.** The BoM put a 60–80%
   probability on a dry winter; a further downgrade would hit PPA order intake.

## 8. Caveats on the data itself

- Revenue is **revenue-recognition basis**, not 8-K segment net sales. Do not mix.
- **All Q4 figures are derived**, not disclosed. They reconcile exactly, but they
  inherit any rounding in the published FY and 9M figures.
- **~26 usable quarters** on the current segment basis, not 30. Any correlation
  computed on this series has n≈26 at best; I have deliberately reported no
  correlation coefficients, because at this sample size against multi-country
  macro drivers they would be very likely spurious.
- Deere discloses **no country-level split** within this six-country geography.
  Every India / Australia / China attribution in this note is inference from
  external data plus management commentary, not disclosure.
- Middle East and Africa market-sizing comes from **commercial market-research
  summaries**, which I rate as low-grade evidence. Used for direction only.
- **Southeast Asia and Africa are genuine gaps** — no quarter-specific data found.
  Blank, not zero.
- The ICRA mid-monsoon PDF (dated 3 Aug 2026) and the USDA FAS GAIN India report
  (Aug 2026) could not be text-extracted directly; figures attributed to them come
  from search-result summaries and should be treated as second-hand.
