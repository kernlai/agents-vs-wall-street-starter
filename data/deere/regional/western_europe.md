on Thursday 20 August 2026. Nothing in this note is an actual. Every FY2026 Q3 figure is my estimate.

tie (Q2 FY2026 PPA: 4,607 footnote vs 4,503 segment table, a 104m gap). Do not mix them when aggregating.

Structural point that makes this region distinctive: **Western Europe is the only Deere geography where
SAT is larger than PPA.** In Q2 FY2026, SAT 827 vs PPA 654. That has been true in most quarters since the
segment reorganisation and reflects small average farm size, the dairy/livestock base, and the fact that
Deere's turf equipment sells "primarily in North American, Western European, and Australian markets"
([FY2025 10-K](../../../challenge/offline-data/deere/filings/2025-12-18__de-us-20251218-fy-10k__393777.md)).
It means European estimates are more sensitive to dairy and turf than to grain prices — a reversal of the
usual Deere reflex.

**Cycle context.** FY2023 was the European peak at 7,321 full-year (vs FY2025 6,550). FY2024 (6,189) and the
first half of FY2025 were a violent destock — Q1 FY2025 at 1,016 was the lowest quarter in the entire
seven-year series. Europe then inflected in Q3 FY2025 (+30.1%) and has grown every quarter since. FY2026
H1 (3,571) is now back within 2% of FY2023 H1 (3,628), i.e. **Western Europe has round-tripped to
peak-cycle H1 revenue levels**. That matters: from here, growth requires new demand, not restocking.

## 3. The FX story — this is the single most important number in my region

I built a translation basket (`scripts/data/weu_fx_basket.py`) weighting EUR 0.80 / GBP 0.12 / SEK 0.08,
averaging FRED daily rates over each Deere 13-week fiscal quarter. Weights are my assumption; Deere does
not disclose a currency split for the geography line.

| Fiscal quarter | EUR/USD avg | Basket YoY | Reported WEu YoY | Implied constant-currency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2025 Q3 | 1.1488 | +6.6% | +30.1% | +22.0% |
| FY2025 Q4 | 1.1677 | +6.0% | +24.7% | +17.7% |
| FY2026 Q1 | 1.1676 | +11.1% | +40.7% | +26.7% |
| FY2026 Q2 | 1.1688 | +8.2% | +17.6% | +8.7% |
| **FY2026 Q3** | **1.1532** | **+0.3%** | *unreported* | — |

The euro rallied hard through mid-2025 and then **stopped**. Because Q3 FY2025 was the quarter the euro
had already re-rated (1.1488 average), the year-over-year translation effect for Q3 FY2026 collapses from
+8.2% to **+0.3%**. Mechanically, roughly eight points of Deere's Western European revenue growth
evaporate in Q3 for reasons that have nothing to do with demand.

Correlation of basket FX YoY against reported Western Europe total YoY across the disclosed period is
**+0.48 (n = 26 quarters)**. With 26 observations and FX cycles that overlap the ag cycle, I would not
treat that as causal evidence — it is consistent with translation mattering, no more. The decomposition
table above is the honest tool, not the correlation.

## 4. Is the Deere share gain real, and did it continue May–July?

Deere's Q2 slide reported April 2026 rolling-3-month Europe Ag retail (Deere internal):
**tractors up double digits, combines up double digits**, against a stated industry outlook of flat to up 5%.

**My verdict: the gain is real, and it probably continued, but the industry backdrop deteriorated
underneath it during exactly the Q3 window.**

Evidence *for* the share gain being real and persisting:

- Deere's own reported Q2 FY2026 Western Europe revenue grew **+8.7% in constant currency** — very close
  to what "retail up low double digits in units, less mix and price" would imply. The retail claim and the
  revenue print corroborate each other.
- **UK tractor registrations H1 2026: 5,955 units, +22.3% YoY**; June alone 999 units, +17% YoY
  ([AEA via Farmers Guardian](https://www.farmersguardian.com/news/4533027/tractor-registrations-22-half-2026)).
  Ireland H1 2026: 1,487 units, **+12%** ([FTMTA via Agriland](https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/ftmta-1487-new-tractors-registered-in-first-half-of-2026/)).
  Both are Western Europe markets running far above the "flat to up 5%" industry line, and both cover
  months inside Deere's Q3.
- Deere took field inventory in Europe down 10–15% through FY2025 and now says European "2026 production
  is largely aligned with retail demand" with "order visibility... through the third quarter and into the
  fourth" ([Q2 FY2026 call](../../../challenge/offline-data/deere/call-transcripts/2026-05-21__de-us-20260521-call-pres__1042774.md)).
  European tractor order books were 4–5 months out as of February 2026 — that booking window lands squarely
  on Q3 shipments. **Q3 European volume was largely sold before the quarter began.** This is the strongest
  single reason to expect Q3 growth despite the sentiment collapse below.

Evidence that the *industry* got materially worse inside the quarter:

- **CEMA business climate index (European ag machinery manufacturers): Apr −6 → May −9 → Jun −20 → Jul −19.**
  June was the sharpest one-month fall and pushed the sector "back into recession after just over a year."
  July "stabilizes in recessionary territory." Sources:
  [April](https://www.cema-agri.org/market-trends/24-business-barometer/1126-april-2026-business-climate-deteriorating-again),
  [May](https://www.cema-agri.org/market-trends/24-business-barometer/1128-may-2026-business-climate-on-the-brink-of-recession),
  [June](https://www.cema-agri.org/market-trends/24-business-barometer/1129-june-2026-business-climate-slips-back-into-recession),
  [July](https://www.cema-agri.org/market-trends/24-business-barometer/1131-july-2026-business-climate-stabilizes-in-recessionary-territory).
- CEMA's July geography read is a clean split: **stable in Germany, UK, Ireland, Scandinavia and the Alpine
  region; weak in France, Italy and Poland.** Germany + UK + Ireland + Nordics is a large majority of
  Deere's Western Europe; France and Italy are the drag.
- CEMA's July segment read: **arable equipment the weakest, livestock above average**, components down
  sharply. Arable maps to Deere PPA; livestock maps to Deere SAT.
- France is genuinely bad. Axema's 2026 report puts French ag-equipment revenue at 0 to −5% for 2026 after
  −9% in 2025, with tractor registrations −15% in 2025 to 33,446 (a ten-year low) and recovery pushed to
  2027 ([Axema via Entraid](https://www.entraid.com/articles/marche-de-la-machine-agricole-2026)).

**Reconciliation:** the CEMA barometer measures *manufacturer sentiment and expected orders over the next
six months*. It is a forward indicator of bookings, not a coincident indicator of shipments. Deere's Q3
shipments were largely covered by an order book taken in Q1/Q2. So I read the June/July collapse as
predominantly a **Q4 FY2026 and FY2027 risk**, with only a modest late-Q3 effect. That is why I stay
positive on Q3 while flagging the setup for the guidance commentary on 20 August as the real risk.

## 5. Segment drivers

### PPA — 695 central, +2.7%
- Arable is CEMA's weakest segment every month from April through July 2026.
- Crop prices are actually *supportive*: global wheat averaged **$210/mt in May–July 2026, +17.8% YoY**;
  maize $206/mt, +4.1% YoY (IMF via FRED). Improving arable cash flow is what management cited in
  November 2025 ("harvest results exceeded expectations in key markets such as France, Germany and Spain").
  Supportive cash flow has not yet translated into arable equipment orders.
- UK's H1 registration growth was concentrated in **241–320hp** with declines above 320hp — larger arable
  operations are not buying the biggest machines. That caps European large-ag upside.
- Combines: Deere's April retail read had European combines up double digits, and Q3 is the European
  harvest-shipment quarter (Zweibrücken). This is the main reason PPA does not go negative.
- Q2→Q3 seasonal ratio history (n=5): mean 1.032, median 1.099, range 0.792–1.203. I apply ~1.06 to the
  Q2 FY2026 actual of 654 → 693. Rounded to 695.

### SAT — 810 central, +7.0%
This is the largest cell in my region and the one I am least comfortable with.
- **The dairy pillar is cracking.** European average standard milk price was **€38.20/100kg in June 2026,
  €10.31/100kg below June 2025 — roughly −21% YoY**
  ([eDairyNews](https://en.edairynews.com/european-milk-prices-fluctuate-as-global-butter-values-decline/)).
  EU SMP at €274/100kg on 23 July 2026 was −46% YoY, though butter at €396 was +15% YoY
  ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/eu-dairy-commodity-prices-butter-up-15-smp-down-46-as-of-july-2026/)).
  Deere has cited "robust dairy margins" or "favourable dairy margins" as the load-bearing support for
  European SAT on every call from Q3 FY2025 through Q2 FY2026. In February 2026 management explicitly said
  "no near-term material impact expected from... recent declines in milk prices." By June that decline is
  −21%. **This is the most under-priced risk in my region.**
- Offsetting: CEMA still ranks livestock equipment above average through July, farmgate milk stopped
  falling in May 2026, and Deere is producing to retail rather than underproducing (management's stated
  driver of the FY2026 SAT +15% guide).
- Q2→Q3 ratio history (n=5): mean 0.946, median 0.920, FY2025 1.135. I apply ~0.98 to 827 → 810.

### CF — 590 central, +7.3%
- Deere raised global roadbuilding to **up ~10%** at Q2 FY2026 (from ~5% at Q1), and management called out
  Wirtgen strength as "not just concentrated in North America... Europe as well."
- The German roadbuilding manufacturing base — Wirtgen (Windhagen), Vögele (Ludwigshafen), Hamm
  (Tirschenreuth), Kleemann (Göppingen) — is all CF, and Q3 is European peak paving season.
- **Euro-area production in construction rose 1.2% YoY in May 2026** (+0.4% MoM; EU +1.8% YoY)
  ([Eurostat, 20 July 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/products-euro-indicators/w/4-20072026-ap)).
  Positive but unspectacular — this supports mid-single-digit, not double-digit, CF growth.
- Global forestry guided down ~5%, a drag on the Nordic CF business (Sweden/Finland).
- Q2→Q3 ratio history (n=5): mean 0.917, median 0.885, FY2025 1.107. I apply ~0.97 to 608 → 590.

### FS — 53 central, +17.8%
Small and unusually well-behaved: 40, 40, 64, 45 / 43, 44, 45, 53 / 54, 52 over the last ten quarters.
Portfolio growth plus higher rates. Q3 FY2025 base of 45 is low. This is the one cell I would call
high-confidence.

## 6. German manufacturing footprint

Per the FY2025 10-K property schedule, Deere's German plants are **Mannheim (SAT and PPA — the 6-series
tractor plant)**, **Zweibrücken (PPA and SAT — combines and forage harvesters)**, plus the four Wirtgen-group
CF plants. Germany long-lived assets rose to **$1,435m from $1,271m** year over year, i.e. Deere is still
investing in the German base, not shrinking it.

I found **no evidence of a production change at Mannheim or Zweibrücken inside the May–July 2026 window.**
The Kurzarbeit (short-time work) episodes I can source at both plants are dated to the 2025 downturn, with
Zweibrücken's works-council "Z³" agreement guaranteeing jobs to early 2027. Given management said European
production is now "largely aligned with retail demand" and order books were full through Q3, a Q3 FY2026
production cut in Germany would be inconsistent with everything else in the record. **Treat this as
"nothing found," not as "confirmed no change"** — German plant-level news is poorly covered by the sources
available to me.

## 7. Policy — CAP, and why I am not putting a number on it

The Commission's July 2025 proposal for CAP 2028–2034 cuts agricultural resources ~20% and merges the two
CAP pillars into a single nationally managed fund. It has drawn sustained protest — roughly 20,000 farmers
from 25 countries in Brussels via Copa-Cogeca. This is a real overhang on European farmer confidence and
plausibly part of why CEMA sentiment broke down in June.

But: the money does not change until 2028, and I have **no evidence of a Q3 FY2026 protest movement
physically disrupting purchasing** the way the winter 2024 blockades did. Deere's Q1 FY2026 call said there
was no near-term material impact expected from newly negotiated EU trade agreements. I therefore treat CAP
as a **sentiment/confidence factor already embedded in the CEMA readings**, and I do not apply a separate
haircut. Double-counting it would be the error here.

## 9. Caveats

1. **No Q3 FY2026 actuals exist.** Every FY2026 Q3 number here is my estimate. Deere reports 20 August 2026.
2. Revenue-recognition basis ≠ segment net-sales basis. Do not mix them in the aggregation.
3. Q4 rows in the history are **derived** (FY minus 9M), not disclosed. They carry rounding from two
   published figures.
4. The FX basket weights (EUR 0.80 / GBP 0.12 / SEK 0.08) are **my assumption**. Deere does not disclose a
   currency split for the Western Europe geography line. A different split changes the constant-currency
   decomposition, though not the direction: EUR, GBP and SEK all show a near-flat YoY for Q3 FY2026.
5. Seasonality ratios rest on **n = 5** four-segment observations (FY2021–FY2025), four of which fall in a
   downcycle. That is a thin base and the observed range (total 0.840–1.115) is wide. The ±270m range on my
   total reflects that, not false precision.
6. The FX/revenue correlation of +0.48 is over **n = 26** quarters with overlapping cycles. Indicative only.
7. CEMA measures **manufacturer sentiment and forward order expectations**, not shipments, and covers all of
   Europe including Central Europe/Poland — it is not a Deere-specific or Western-Europe-specific series.
8. UK and Irish registration data are unit registrations, not Deere revenue, and are not brand-split (UK
   brand data is published 12 months in arrears for competition reasons).
9. I could not source Q2/Q3 2026 country-level registration data for Germany, France, Italy or Spain from
   CEMA — those are absent rows in the CSV rather than estimates.
10. Dairy and Axema figures come from trade press rather than primary statistical releases. I have cited
    them with URLs and dates; treat them as one notch below the Eurostat/FRED series.
