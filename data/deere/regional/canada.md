## 2. Are Canada and the US diverging in 2026? **Yes — materially, and in both directions.**

Deere publishes one industry outlook for "U.S. and Canada large ag: down 15–20%". Canada is roughly
10–15% of that combined unit pool but a much larger share of the 4WD articulated-tractor and air-seeder
pool, so a Canadian divergence is not visible in the blended number.

**Ag: the US retail rebound is not happening in Canada.** Deere's own Q2 FY2026 deck
(`slides/2026-05-21__de-us-20260521-slide__1042212.md`, April 2026 rolling-3-month) shows *U.S. and
Canada* industry retail up across the board: 100+ hp 2WD +14%, 4WD +24%, combines +5%. AEM's
Canada-only report tells the opposite story:

- Canadian ag tractor units: **−12.5% YoY in June 2026**, **≈ −8% in July 2026**
- Canadian combines: **+14.2% in June 2026**, **≈ −11% in July 2026**
- Canada YTD through July 2026: **4WD −22.6%**, **2WD −8.9%**, **combines −1.9%**

So the +24% 4WD figure in Deere's blended table is being carried by the United States while Canadian
4WD retail is down more than twenty percent year to date. This is the clearest evidence of divergence
in the dataset, and it is a direct negative for Canadian PPA shipments.

**Farm economics: Canada is diverging *upward*.** Prairie crop economics improved sharply through the
Q3 window on a canola-specific catalyst that has no US analogue:

| Prairie avg farm price, CAD/t | May-25 | Jun-25 | Jul-25 | … | Feb-26 | Mar-26 | Apr-26 | May-26 | Jun-26 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Canola | 652 | 669 | 677 | | 638 | 657 | 670 | **693** | **706** |
| Wheat ex-durum | 305 | 307 | 304 | | 268 | 274 | 284 | 292 | 296 |
| Durum | 345 | 349 | 348 | | 279 | 285 | 286 | 288 | 292 |
| Barley | 276 | 274 | 267 | | 244 | 262 | 256 | 261 | 268 |

(Statistics Canada Table 32-10-0077, mean of SK/AB/MB; July 2026 not yet published.)

Canola is **+6.3% YoY in May 2026 and +5.6% in June 2026**, and at its highest level since January 2024.
The driver is the China settlement: after the 16 January 2026 Canada–China agreement-in-principle,
MOFCOM cut the canola-seed anti-dumping duty from the 75.8% preliminary rate to **5.9%** (final
determination 28 Feb 2026, effective 1 Mar 2026; 14.9% all-in with the 9% MFN duty) and suspended the
100% canola-meal tariff to **0%** for 1 Mar – 31 Dec 2026. Canadian growers responded immediately:
2026 seeded canola area is **23.44m acres, +8.4% YoY** and the largest since 2022, while all-wheat area
fell 5.6% and durum 10.3% (StatCan 32-10-0359, June 2026 survey). Durum and wheat prices are still
down 15–17% and 4% YoY respectively, so this is a canola story, not a broad grain story.

**Weather: a normal-to-good prairie year, not a drought year.** June 2026 was very wet (Swan River MB
270 mm; multiple prairie locations over 200 mm, some flooding and drowned canola stands losing 20–30%).
July flipped hot and dry — southern AB and SK saw under 40% of normal precipitation with temperatures
3–5 °C above normal — but spring subsoil moisture carried the crop and only about **2% of the prairie
agricultural land base was in drought** at the end of July. Southern Alberta was still 79.8% good-to-
excellent on major crops as of 28 July. Prairie drought is **not** a differentiating negative this year;
if anything the June rain was the bigger agronomic event.

**Net read on the divergence:** Canadian *farm income fundamentals* are improving faster than the US
(canola price + acreage + China reopening), but Canadian *equipment retail* is materially weaker than
the US. Those two pull in opposite directions, and the income improvement arrives with a lag —
farmers book equipment after the crop is in the bin and priced. That argues the canola tailwind shows
up in Deere's Canadian Q4 FY2026 / Q1 FY2027 order book rather than in Q3 FY2026 shipments.

## 4. Construction & Forestry: the Canadian anomaly

Company CF grew +29% in Q2 FY2026 and is guided up ~20% for the year. Canadian CF fell **−15.9%** in
Q2 FY2026 (175 vs 208) and is **+0.6%** across H1. Canada's share of global CF revenue has collapsed:

| Canada share of global CF (rev-rec) | FY2022 Q3 | FY2023 Q3 | FY2024 Q3 | FY2025 Q3 | FY2026 Q1 | FY2026 Q2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| | 8.67% | 7.59% | 5.56% | 7.10% | 4.97% | 4.54% |

In absolute terms Canadian CF went 1,221 (FY2023) → 760 (FY2024) → 743 (FY2025) — a 39% peak-to-trough
fall that has not recovered while the rest of the world's CF business inflected upward.

The forestry explanation holds up against independent data:
- **Canadian lumber production is falling.** StatCan 16-10-0017: total production −5.8% YoY in April 2026
  and −8.1% in May 2026; FY-to-date 2026 running −5% to −12% every month. Full-year 2025 was already
  below 2024.
- **US trade action is the cause.** Combined AD/CVD duties on Canadian softwood were reset to ~35.2%
  in summer 2025, and a further 10% Section 232 tariff on softwood plus 25% on derivative wood products
  landed in mid-October 2025. BC announced a stumpage-deferral programme in February 2026 to keep mills
  running; the seventh administrative review is due to finalise rates as early as **August 2026**, i.e.
  inside or just after the Q3 window, so no relief landed during the quarter.
- Deere guides **global forestry down ~5%** for FY2026 (down from "flat" at Q1) — and Canada is one of
  the two or three largest forestry-equipment markets in the world, so it absorbs a disproportionate
  share of that decline.

Offsetting: global roadbuilding was raised to up ~10%, and Deere's North American earthmoving order
book is "up more than 60% since November, now at its highest level since April of 2024, with over 80%
of production slots filled" (Q2 FY2026 call). Canadian earthmoving participates in that, but Wirtgen
roadbuilding volumes in Canada are small relative to the US and Europe, so the mix lift is muted.

## 5. Canadian farm credit, income and support programmes

- **Farm cash receipts (StatCan 32-10-0046, calendar quarters, unadjusted).** Latest published quarter
  is **2026 Q1** — there is no Q2 2026 data yet, so the Q3 window is not directly covered. Canada total
  Q1 2026 was **CAD 25,085m, −1.6% YoY**; total crop receipts **12,503m, −3.2%**. Saskatchewan crop
  receipts bucked the trend at **+4.8%** (canola marketings +CAD 476m nationally, per StatCan's
  27 May 2026 release), while Alberta crop receipts fell 11.3%. Calendar-2025 Canadian farm cash
  receipts were CAD 101.4bn, +3.4%.
- **Programme support.** The AgriStability compensation rate was lifted from 80% to 90% and the payment
  cap doubled to CAD 6m for the 2025 programme year. The interest-free portion of the Advance Payments
  Program for canola advances was raised to CAD 500,000 for the 2025 and 2026 programme years
  (Sept 2025) — directly relevant liquidity support for prairie growers through the 2026 season. FPT
  ministers signed the "Halifax Statement" on 20 July 2026 promising a further doubling of the
  AgriStability cap and recognition of family labour, but **for 2027 implementation** — no cash effect
  inside Q3 FY2026.
- **Credit conditions.** Canadian operating-loan rates have eased modestly with Bank of Canada cuts but
  remain historically elevated, and longer-term/land financing costs are sticky. I could not find a
  Canada-specific FCC 2026 outlook figure I was willing to cite, so I have left that as a qualitative
  point rather than putting a number in the CSV.

## 6. Canada–US trade friction in 2026

- **Equipment tariffs eased mid-quarter.** A US proclamation of 1 June 2026 cut the Section 232
  derivative rate on **agricultural equipment** from 25% to **15%, effective 8 June 2026** through
  31 December 2027. CUSMA-qualifying mobile industrial equipment is taxed on non-US content only,
  subject to a 15%-of-total-value floor. This lands *inside* the Q3 window and is a modest cost relief
  for cross-border equipment flows — Deere's Q2 call put full-year tariff expense at ~3 points of
  equipment-operations margin, with ~20% of the exposure in large ag.
- **Broader friction is worsening.** Trump said on 11 June 2026 he is "not looking to renew" CUSMA
  before the 1 July review deadline; Canada's trade minister described bilateral arrangements as now
  "adjacent to" the trilateral framework. Canada maintains 25% retaliatory tariffs on US steel,
  aluminium and autos. A further US tariff tranche at 50% takes effect 19 August 2026 — *after* the
  quarter closed, so it is a Q4 FY2026 issue, not Q3.
- **Softwood lumber** remains the sharpest Canada-specific dispute (see §4).

Net: tariffs were a small *tailwind* to Canadian equipment economics inside Q3 FY2026 and a
*headwind* to Canadian forestry demand. Trade-policy uncertainty is the bigger effect and it suppresses
capital-goods orders on both sides of the border.

## 7. Correlations — with sample sizes, and with a health warning

Computed on the 22 overlapping new-basis quarters from FY2021 Q1 onward. **n = 22 is a small sample
spanning one enormous boom (FY2021–23) and one bust (FY2024–25), and the crop-price series are highly
collinear with each other.** I regard these as suggestive of a lag structure, not as estimable
coefficients, and I have not used any of them to size the forecast.

| Pair | r | n |
|---|---:|---:|
| Canada PPA YoY vs prairie wheat ex-durum price YoY, lag 2 quarters | +0.79 | 22 |
| Canada PPA YoY vs prairie durum price YoY, lag 2 quarters | +0.71 | 22 |
| Canada PPA YoY vs prairie canola price YoY, lag 2 quarters | +0.68 | 22 |
| Canada TOTAL YoY vs prairie canola price YoY, lag 2 quarters | +0.69 | 22 |
| Canada PPA YoY vs prairie canola price YoY, same quarter | +0.31 | 22 |
| Canada PPA YoY vs USD/CAD YoY, same quarter | −0.29 | 22 |

The only robust reading: **crop prices lead Canadian PPA revenue by roughly two quarters**, and the
same-quarter relationship is weak. Applied naively, the two-quarter lag says FY2026 Q3 should be
predicted by Nov-25–Jan-26 prices, when canola was flat YoY and wheat/durum were down 8–13% — a
negative signal. The canola rally only began in March 2026 and therefore, on this lag structure,
supports **Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027, not Q3**. The negative sign on USD/CAD is the wrong way round for a
translation story and is almost certainly spurious (the CAD was strong during the FY2021–22 revenue
boom); do not use it.

## 8. Q3 FY2026 Canada forecast

**Method.** Two independent routes, reconciled:
1. **Share-of-global.** Estimate global Q3 FY2026 rev-rec revenue by segment from FY2026 guidance and
   management's stated H2 cadence, then apply a Canada share informed by (a) the Q3-specific seasonal
   share and (b) the FY2026 H1 share drift versus FY2025 H1.
2. **Direct YoY** on the Q3 FY2025 Canada comparative, informed by Canadian retail, FX and prices.

**Global anchor used** (8-K basis → rev-rec via the ~2.3–2.6% observed uplift). FY2026 guidance:
PPA −5 to −10% on 17,311; SAT ~+15% on 10,224; CF ~+20% on 11,382. H1 FY2026 actuals: PPA 7,666,
SAT 5,653, CF 6,460. Management (Q2 call): H2 > H1 overall; large ag Q4 "a bit stronger than Q3" with
"more Waterloo large tractor shipments shipping to North America in the back half… that's abnormal for
us"; SAT normal seasonality, "a little bit of a step down in Q3 and another step down in Q4"; C&F
"fairly balanced" with Q4 slightly ahead. That yields global Q3 FY2026 rev-rec ≈ PPA 4,110, SAT 3,410,
CF 3,590, FS ~1,400. **If the aggregator's global numbers differ, rescale my Canada cells on the
implied shares rather than the dollars.**

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| PPA | 335 | **340** | **+1.5%** | 295 | 390 | 8.3% | low |

**PPA — 340 (+1.5%), low confidence.** Pulling up: an unusually soft base (Q3 FY2025 fell 31.5%, double
the company decline); the explicitly abnormal H2 skew of Waterloo large-tractor shipments to North
America; lean Deere dealer inventories (US&Canada combines at 12% of trailing retail vs 17% LY);
canola price and acreage strength. Pulling down: Canadian retail units genuinely weak and weaker than
the US (4WD −22.6% YTD; tractors −8% in July); the Q2 FY2026 print of −25.8%; the 10-Q's own MD&A
attributing the PPA decline to "lower shipment volumes (primarily in the U.S., Canada, and Brazil)"; a
~1.6% FX headwind; the two-quarter crop-price lag says the canola benefit arrives after this quarter.
Canada's Q3 share of global PPA has run 5.70–9.33% over four years (mean ~7.5%); I use 8.3%, above the
mean because of the Waterloo skew. **The distribution here is genuinely wide — a 295 or a 390 would not
surprise me.**

**SAT — 175 (+18.2%), medium confidence.** Canada SAT is +24.1% in H1 on dairy/livestock margins
(strong beef prices) and a turf recovery, and Canada's SAT share has drifted up (H1 FY2026 4.91% vs
4.70% H1 FY2025). Management flags normal seasonality — a step down from the Q2 187. Applying a 5.1%
share to a global 3,410 gives 175. The main risk is that Q3 FY2024's 207 shows Canada SAT can spike.

**CF — 200 (−9.9%), low confidence.** This is the most contrarian call in the note: I am forecasting
Canadian CF **down** in a quarter when company CF should be up ~15%. Justification: Canada's CF share
has fallen from 5.92% (H1 FY2025) to 4.76% (H1 FY2026), a 20% relative decline; Canadian lumber
production is −8.1% YoY in May 2026 with 35%+ US duties and Section 232 tariffs unresolved through the
quarter; Deere cut global forestry to −5%; and Canada is over-indexed to forestry within CF. Straight
share-reversion to 5.68% of a 3,590 global gives 204. The bull case is that Canada participates in the
+60% North American earthmoving order book and prints 240 (+8%); the bear case is continued share loss
to ~4.7% and a 170 (−23%).

**FS — 196 (+3.2%), medium confidence.** The most stable series in the region: Canada FS has printed
between 145 and 212 for 26 straight quarters and 186–212 for the last twelve. Share is 12.4–13.9% of
global FS and drifting up (13.80%, 13.90% in FY2026 H1). Portfolio growth and higher earned finance
income offset a flat global FS line.

**Total 911 (+1.8%).** For context this would make FY2026 Canada ≈ 826 + 1,039 + 911 + ~1,000 ≈ 3,776,
roughly +1% on FY2025's 3,735 — consistent with a region where a large-ag decline is being offset by
SAT strength and where CF is no longer falling as fast as it did in FY2024.

## 9. Risks to this view

1. **CF is the biggest single risk.** A ±35m swing in Canadian CF is ±4% of the regional total, and my
   confidence there is low. If Canadian earthmoving simply tracks the North American order book, CF
   comes in at 240 and the region prints ~950.
2. **PPA timing.** Deere's Canadian shipment pattern is lumpy quarter to quarter (Q2 FY2026 −25.8% right
   after Q1 FY2026 +12.4%). The Waterloo H2 skew could land disproportionately in Q4, taking Q3 PPA to
   ~300 and the region to ~870.
3. **No Q3 macro data exists yet.** StatCan farm cash receipts stop at 2026 Q1; farm product prices stop
   at June 2026; lumber production stops at May 2026. The last month of the quarter is unobserved.
4. **The AEM Canada figures are trade-press restatements**, not the AEM PDFs themselves (aem.org PDFs
   and realagriculture.com returned 403). They should be treated as approximately right, not exact.
5. **FX is a modelling simplification.** I treat CAD translation as ~−1 point; the actual Canada-geography
   revenue is a mix of CAD- and USD-invoiced business and Deere does not disclose the split.
6. **The 19 August 2026 US tariff tranche** and the softwood administrative-review determination both
   land after the quarter but before/around the print, so management commentary on 20 August may be
   more negative on Canada than the Q3 numbers themselves.
