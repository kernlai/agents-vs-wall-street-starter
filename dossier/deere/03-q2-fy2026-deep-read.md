### The $272 m IEEPA tariff refund — the single biggest one-off in the quarter
**REPORTED FACT.** 10-Q L1880: *"On February 20, 2026, the Supreme Court of the United States issued a decision invalidating tariffs imposed pursuant to the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). On April 20, 2026, the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) launched a system to process IEEPA tariff refund claims… we prepared and filed a refund claim in the amount of $272, which has been accepted by the CBP. We recorded a recovery for this initial amount as we concluded the refund is probable and reasonably estimable. **The recovery was allocated 20%, 30%, and 50% to PPA, SAT, and CF, respectively, decreasing cost of sales.**"*

⇒ **PPA got ~$54 m, SAT ~$82 m, CF ~$136 m** (*inference* from the 20/30/50 split).
⇒ **PPA's clean, ex-refund Q2 operating profit ≈ $652 m; margin ≈ 14.5%** (*inference*).
Management (call, `call-transcripts/2026-05-21__de-us-20260521-call-pres__1042774.md` L75): the refund *"benefited our production cost this quarter and lifted margins by nearly 2.5 points."*
Q&A L29 gives the same split as ~50% CF / ~30% SAT / ~20% large ag.

**This is a Q3 forecasting trap:** the refund is described as an *initial* claim under CBP's *initial phase*. Whether a second tranche lands in Q3 is unstated. Management's FY guide nets the $272 m against the $1.2 bn run-rate to reach **~$900 m of tariff cost for the year** — i.e. **no further refund is embedded in guidance**. Any additional Q3 refund would be pure upside; the base case should assume none.

## 9. Two mechanical traps to carry into the model

1. **The 53rd week is in Q4, not Q3 — Q3 is a clean comp, Q4 is not.** 10-Q L400 (REPORTED FACT): *"We use a 52/53 week fiscal year with quarters ending on the last Sunday in the reporting period. The second quarter ends for fiscal years 2026 and 2025 were May 3, 2026, and April 27, 2025, respectively. **Both quarters contained 13 weeks**, while both year-to-date periods contained 26 weeks. **Fiscal year 2025 contained 53 weeks, with the additional week occurring in the fourth quarter.**"* (Corroborated by `filings/2025-11-26__de-us-20251126-q4-10k__469216.md` L4845.)
   - ⇒ Q2 FY26's +5% is a clean 13w-vs-13w comparison; **no extra-week distortion to unwind.**
   - ⇒ **Q3 FY2026 (4 May – ~2 Aug 2026, 13 weeks) vs Q3 FY2025 (28 Apr – 27 Jul 2025, 13 weeks) is also a clean comp.** Good news for the Q3 model.
   - ⇒ **Q4 FY2026 will be 13 weeks vs Q4 FY2025's 14 weeks** — a ~7% week-count headwind on the Q4 y/y comp. This matters for the H2 split: FY2025's Q3 share of H2 revenue (49.2%) is *understated* because that year's Q4 carried an extra week. Normalising FY2025 Q4 to 13 weeks (12,394 × 13/14 ≈ $11,509 m) lifts the like-for-like Q3 share of H2 to **~51%** (*inference*). Management's explicit *"Q4 higher than Q3"* guidance for FY2026 therefore has to overcome a lost week — it is a stronger statement about Q4 volumes than it first appears, and correspondingly a **firmer signal that Q3 sits below 50% of H2**. The 47–49% Q3 share used in §8 already reflects this.
2. **Effective tax rate.** Q2 actual was **22.6%** and H1 **22.8%**, both *below* the 24–26% FY guide. To land inside the guide, H2 tax must run **~25–28%** (*inference*: to hit a 25% FY rate on the mid-guide, H2 rate must be ~26.5%). A higher Q3 tax rate is therefore a modelled headwind to Q3 EPS relative to the H1 run-rate. Do not roll H1's 22.8% forward.
