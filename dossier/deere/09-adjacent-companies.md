## 0. Executive read-across in five lines

1. **Peer OEM shipments into North American ag inflected positive in Apr–Jun 2026** — AGCO NA net sales **+19.7%**, CNH NA agriculture net sales **+10%** — even though industry *retail* was still falling hard. Both are lapping deep 2025 underproduction. `[FACT]`
2. **Industry retail in Deere's exact FQ3 window (May–Jul 2026) was ugly: US total farm tractors ≈ −17.3% YoY, 100+hp still double-digit negative, 4WD −38.7% in July.** `[FACT]/[INFER]`
3. **The single largest cross-peer P&L surprise is IEEPA tariff refunds** (SCOTUS struck IEEPA tariffs 20 Feb 2026). AGCO, Caterpillar, Kubota and Amazon all recognised refunds in the Apr–Jun window — *after* Deere's FQ2 closed on 3 May. Deere already took $272M in FQ2. A further refund in FQ3 is the biggest single upside risk to Deere's FQ3 GAAP EPS. `[FACT]/[INFER]`
4. **Correlation is high but the peers are confirmatory, not leading.** Deere fiscal-quarter revenue growth vs. the best-overlapping peer calendar quarter: **CNH r = 0.96**, **AGCO r = 0.85** (n = 15, FY23Q1–FY26Q2). Shifting the peer window *earlier* (i.e. treating peers as a leading indicator) **degrades** the fit. Peers tell you what the cycle is doing; they do not front-run Deere's print.
5. **Correcting the brief:** Titan Machinery is a **CNH Industrial (Case IH / New Holland) dealer, not a Deere dealer.** There is no publicly listed pure Deere dealer (Cervus was taken private in 2021). Titan is still the best public dealer-channel signal, but it reads across to CNH first and Deere second. Titan's FQ2 (ended 31 Jul 2026 — the closest match to Deere's FQ3 of any listed company) reports **27 August 2026, one week after Deere** ([GlobeNewswire, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/13/3344323/0/en/titan-machinery-inc-to-report-fiscal-second-quarter-ended-july-31-2026-results-on-thursday-august-27-2026.html)) — so it will not help before the print.

## 4. The IEEPA tariff-refund thread — the biggest cross-peer earnings surprise

The US Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on **20 February 2026** ([Skadden, Feb 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/02/the-supreme-court-ends-ieepa-tariffs)); the Court of International Trade ordered CBP to refund roughly **$165bn** of collected IEEPA duties across 330,000+ importers ([Steptoe global-trade blog](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/status-of-ieepa-tariff-refunds-following-us-supreme-court-decision.html); [Skadden, Apr 2026, on the CAPE refund mechanism](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/04/insights-april-2026/tariff-refund-mechanism-takes-shape)).

| Company | Refund recognised | Window |
|---|---|---|
| **Deere** | **$272M** | FQ2 FY26 (Feb–3 May 26) `[FACT, corpus QnA line 29]` |
| AGCO | $22M | Apr–Jun 26 `[FACT]` |
| Caterpillar | IEEPA recoveries cut quarterly tariff cost to ~$400M vs ~$700M planned (~$300M benefit) | Apr–Jun 26 `[FACT]` |
| Kubota | "U.S. tariff refunds" cited as a driver of +64.7% H1 OP | Jan–Jun 26 `[FACT]` |
| Amazon | $640M | Apr–Jun 26 `[FACT, [Roic News, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.roic.ai/news/amazon-discloses-640-million-in-ieepa-tariff-refunds-in-q2-2026-filing-07-31-2026)]` |

**`[INFER]` — the read-across:** refunds were still being recognised across the peer set in the **Apr–Jun window, i.e. after Deere's FQ2 closed on 3 May 2026.** Deere characterised its $272M as one-time, but the industry pattern is of refunds arriving in tranches as CAPE declarations clear. **A further Deere refund in FQ3 is a live, unmodelled upside to GAAP EPS.** For scale: $272M pre-tax ≈ $0.75/share after tax at a 25% rate on 270.8M diluted shares; ~20% of any such refund would land in PPA operating profit. This is the largest single asymmetry in the FQ3 print and it is invisible in industry retail data.

## 5. Quantifying the read-across: how correlated are the peers with Deere?

### 5.1 Method
Deere's fiscal quarter n ends ~one month after the peers' calendar quarter n−1 (Deere FQ3 = May–Jul; AGCO/CNH Q2 = Apr–Jun; 2 of 3 months overlap). I therefore pair **Deere fiscal Qn with peer calendar Q(n−1)** and correlate **YoY revenue growth rates** (levels are non-stationary and seasonal; growth rates are the right object).

- Deere quarterly worldwide net sales & revenues and PPA net sales: corpus 8-Ks (`filings/*-q[1-4]-8k*.md`), FY2020–FY2026Q2.
- AGCO and CNH quarterly revenue: [stockanalysis.com/stocks/agco/financials/?p=quarterly](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/agco/financials/?p=quarterly) and [stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnh/financials/?p=quarterly](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnh/financials/?p=quarterly), CQ3 2021 – CQ2 2026. Peer growth-rate history begins CQ3 2022, giving **n = 15** paired observations (Deere FY23Q1 → FY26Q2).

### 5.2 Correlations `[INFER — my computation, method above]`

| Pair | Contemporaneous (Deere FQn ↔ peer CQn−1) | Peer window shifted 1q **earlier** ("peer leads") | Peer window shifted 1q **later** ("peer lags") |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deere total NSR vs **AGCO** revenue | **r = 0.85** | r = 0.63 | r = 0.97 |
| Deere total NSR vs **CNH** revenue | **r = 0.96** | r = 0.80 | r = 0.94 |
| Deere **PPA** net sales vs AGCO | **r = 0.78** | — | — |
| Deere **PPA** net sales vs CNH | **r = 0.93** | — | — |

**Direction and lead/lag.** Correlation is high and positive, but treating the peers as a *leading* indicator **destroys** the fit (AGCO 0.85 → 0.63; CNH 0.96 → 0.80). Pushing the peer window *later* preserves or improves it. Conclusion: **there is no peer lead. If anything Deere's fiscal quarter leads AGCO's calendar quarter by ~one quarter**, which is what you would expect from Deere's earlier fiscal calendar and its position as the share leader that sets the production cadence. A peer beat/miss is a same-cycle confirmation, not a predictor.

**Does a peer beat/miss predict a Deere beat/miss?** Not reliably, and the mechanism is why. All three companies are exposed to the same cycle, so *revenue level* co-moves. But beats and misses are measured against **separately-set consensus** and are driven by (a) each company's own production plan vs. retail, (b) region mix (AGCO is 66% EME, CNH has a large construction and financial-services book, Deere is NA-heavy in PPA and has a 20%-of-revenue captive finance arm), and (c) one-offs (this quarter: IEEPA refunds, FX). The current quarter is the perfect illustration: **AGCO cut guidance and CNH raised it, in the same three months, in the same industry.** Any claim that a peer's beat mechanically predicts Deere's should be rejected.
