## 1. Bottom line for the forecast

1. **Crop prices were materially HIGHER year-over-year through the quarter** — corn +19.6%, soybeans +15.2%, wheat +33.2% versus a year earlier as of 14 Aug 2026 `[FACT]`. This is the first genuinely favourable price backdrop in three years.
2. **But grower margins did not improve, because the cost side blew out.** The Feb-2026 Iran / Strait-of-Hormuz shock drove Brent from ~$76 to ~$126/bbl in March `[FACT]`; US on-highway diesel was **$5.313/gal in the week of 27 Jul 2026** `[FACT]` and the World Bank fertiliser index hit its highest level since Oct 2022 in April 2026 `[FACT]`. Urea was up ~37–45% y/y in mid-2026 `[FACT]`.
3. **Farmer capital-spending intent stayed at cycle lows through the quarter.** The Purdue/CME Farm Capital Investment Index read **41 (May), 40 (June), 50 (July)** — May/June the lowest since September 2024 `[FACT]`.
4. **Retail equipment demand confirms it:** US 100+hp tractor sales **−15.5% YTD**, 4WD tractors **−38.7% in July**, total tractors **−13.1% YTD** through July 2026 `[FACT]`.
5. **Headline US net farm income is a misleading indicator this cycle.** 2026F net farm income of $153.4bn is roughly flat, but $44.3bn of it (≈29%) is direct government payments; ex-payments it is $109.1bn, ~12% lower `[FACT]`. Large-ag demand tracks *row-crop cash margins*, not the government-augmented headline `[INF]`.
6. **Farmer-liquidity timing is a specific Q3 negative.** The $11–12bn Farmer Bridge Assistance cash landed in bank accounts **by 28 Feb 2026** — Deere's FY26 Q1/Q2 `[FACT]`. The next big tranche (2025-crop ARC/PLC) does not pay until **October 2026** — Deere's FY26 Q4. May–July 2026 was an air-pocket for on-farm cash `[INF]`.
7. **South America is the largest negative delta versus the start of Deere's year.** Deere cut its South American tractor/combine industry guide from −5% to **−15%** at Q2 and explicitly blamed the Iran situation, higher input costs, high Brazilian rates and a stronger real `[FACT]`.
8. **FX is a real positive offset to reported PPA revenue.** Deere booked ~+3 points of currency translation on PPA net sales in FY26 Q2 and guides ~+3 points for the full year `[FACT]` — a weaker dollar / stronger real converts an ugly volume picture into a less ugly revenue line `[INF]`.

**Net read-across:** the macro is consistent with Deere *holding* its FY26 PPA guide (net sales −5% to −10%, margin 11–13%) rather than raising it, with volume risk skewed down in South America and cost/tariff risk skewed up, partially offset by FX. See §10.

## 5. Crop input costs

### 5.1 The shock: Iran / Strait of Hormuz

`[FACT]` The US and Israel began strikes on Iran **28 February 2026**; Iran attacked shipping and effectively shut the Strait of Hormuz. Brent rose ~65% in March, from ~$76 to ~$126/bbl. At the peak diesel traded near $200/bbl. By 1 June 2026 US average diesel was **$5.35/gal**, roughly $2.00/gal above pre-war levels. By late June partial reopening brought WTI back near $95 and Brent near $80.
Sources: [Brookings, "From chokepoint to crisis"](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/from-chokepoint-to-crisis-the-strait-of-hormuz-and-global-oil-markets/); [EIA press release, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.eia.gov/pressroom/releases/press589.php); [usbaseline.com, crude oil 2026](https://usbaseline.com/blog/crude-oil-prices-forecast-2026-hormuz-crisis).

**This is the single most important macro event in the quarter being forecast, and it hit the cost side of the farm P&L, not the revenue side.** `[INF]`

### 6.2 The revision — this is the important part `[FACT]`

USDA's **September 2025** forecast for calendar-2025 net farm income was **$179.8bn**. The **February 2026** estimate cut that to **$154.6bn — a $25.2bn (−14.0%) downward revision.** The American Farm Bureau characterised the revisions as indicating "a generational downturn rather than a temporary slowdown."

`[INF]` This matters for the Deere forecast in two ways:
- Anyone (sell-side or Deere) who built a FY2026 demand plan off the Sept-2025 income picture was working from a number that turned out to be 14% too high. That is part of why the FY26 large-ag industry guide has been progressively cut.
- ERS forecasts have a documented downward bias in *early* vintages and get revised in both directions; the 3 Sept 2026 update is a genuine wildcard for post-Q3 commentary, arriving ~2 weeks after Deere reports.

### 6.3 Government payments are doing the work

`[FACT]` Government payments are ~29–30% of 2026 net farm income. `[INF]` Two consequences:
1. **The headline NFI series has partially decoupled from the row-crop margin that drives large-ag equipment.** Cattle receipts (+$5.2bn) and transfer payments (+$13.8bn) are holding up NFI while corn/soy/wheat growers — the buyers of high-horsepower tractors, combines and planters — remain at or below breakeven.
2. **Direct payments are lumpy in time.** See §7.

⚠️ **Vintage warning:** ERS revises NFI heavily (2025 moved $25bn in five months). The pre-2023 figures above are early-vintage nominal values and are not a consistent series. Treat the elasticity below as an order-of-magnitude relationship, not a calibrated coefficient.

### 9.2 The lag

`[INF]` **PPA peaked in FY2023, one year after NFI peaked in CY2022.** The trough in NFI vintages (2024) is followed by the PPA trough in FY2025/FY2026. **The lag is approximately one year — four quarters.** The mechanism is straightforward: a grower's income in harvest year *t* funds the equipment order placed in the autumn early-order programme of year *t*, which Deere builds and ships in fiscal year *t+1*. Deere's own order-book language on the Q2 FY26 call ("order visibility in both regions now extends through the third quarter and into the fourth") confirms a two-to-three-quarter build-to-ship pipeline.

### 9.3 The elasticity, and why it has broken

`[INF]` Using a one-year lag (ΔNFI in year *t* → ΔPPA net sales in FY *t+1*):

| ΔNFI | period | ΔPPA (t+1) | period | implied amplification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +47.6% | 2020→2021 | +33.3% | FY21→FY22 | 0.70× |
| +29.0% | 2021→2022 | +21.8% | FY22→FY23 | 0.75× |
| −19.5% | 2022→2023 | −22.2% | FY23→FY24 | **1.14×** |
| −4.4% | 2023→2024 | −16.9% | FY24→FY25 | 3.8× (breaks) |
| +10.4% | 2024→2025 | −5 to −10% (guide) | FY25→FY26 | **sign flips (breaks)** |

**Rule of thumb where it holds:** a 1% change in US net farm income maps to roughly **0.7–1.2% change in Deere PPA net sales one year later**, with the downside amplified relative to the upside (equipment is deferrable; a farmer can run an old combine one more season).

**Why the relationship has broken since 2024** `[INF]` — and this is the analytically important point:

1. **Composition.** The 2025/2026 "recovery" in headline NFI is government payments (+$13.8bn in 2026) and cattle receipts (+$5.2bn). Neither buys row-crop iron. Ranchers with record cattle prices buy utility tractors and hay equipment — which shows up in **Small Ag & Turf**, not PPA. That is exactly the split Deere is reporting: SAT net sales **+16% in FY26 Q2** and guided **+15% for the full year**, while PPA is guided **−5% to −10%**. The NFI headline is being carried by the segment of agriculture that buys Deere's *other* product line.
2. **Level vs. change.** Row-crop growers are in year four of negative-to-breakeven margins. Cumulative balance-sheet damage, not the annual delta, governs a $900k combine decision.
3. **Cost inflation is not in NFI's numerator the way it hits capex.** A $5.30 diesel and +37% urea both raise the operating-cash hurdle a farmer must clear before financing new iron, and both raise Deere's own production cost.
4. **Interest rates.** Deere cited "elevated interest rates" in every region on the Q2 call. The cost of financing is a separate, additive drag not captured by NFI.

**Better high-frequency proxies for PPA demand** `[INF]`, in descending order of usefulness:
- Purdue **Farm Capital Investment Index** (monthly, direct question about large machinery purchases) — averaged ~44 in the quarter, cycle-low territory.
- **AEM 100+hp and 4WD retail units** (monthly) — −15.5% YTD and −38.7% in July.
- **Crop cash receipts minus production expenses** (annual, ERS) rather than net farm income.
- New-crop board price relative to published breakeven (Southern Ag Today: breakeven yield > projected yield for corn and soybeans in 2026).

### 10.2 Macro deltas since 21 May 2026 that a forecaster should weigh `[INF]`

**Negative for PPA volume**
- 4WD retail −38.7% in July; 100+hp −15.5% YTD — retail running at/below the bottom of the −15/−20% guide.
- Farm Capital Investment Index at 40–41 in May/June, cycle lows.

**Positive / offsetting**
- Currency: ~+3 points of FX translation on PPA net sales, driven by dollar weakness and a stronger real — the same real strength that hurts Brazilian growers *helps* Deere's reported revenue `[INF]`.
- Crop prices up 15–33% y/y with corn stocks-to-use at ~10.1% and the tightest US corn ending stocks in two years — supportive for FY2027 order books and for management tone.
- July sentiment inflection (barometer 113→126, FCI 40→50), though it arrived in the last three weeks of the quarter.
- China's ≥$17bn/yr ag purchase commitment from the 18 May summit; soybean purchases stepped up into August.
- Fertiliser prices falling steadily from late June — margin relief for the 2027 crop budget.
- Deere is deliberately **underproducing retail demand in Brazil, notably combines**, and Europe production is aligned with retail — so channel inventory is not the problem it was in FY24/25 `[FACT]`, which supports margin even on weak volume.

### 10.3 Directional conclusion `[INF]`

The macro backdrop for May–July 2026 is **"better prices, worse margins, unchanged-to-weaker equipment demand."** It does **not** support an upward revision to the PPA volume line, and the AEM 4WD collapse is a genuine downside risk to the FY26 PPA sales guide's lower bound. It *does* support (a) a constructive FY2027 order-book narrative on the call, and (b) a revenue line flattered by ~3 points of FX. For PPA operating profit specifically, the tension is between weak volume/mix (negative) and price realisation + FX + disciplined production (positive); nothing in the macro argues for margin outside the 11–13% full-year guide corridor.
