### 4a. FTC / five-state antitrust "right to repair" suit — SETTLED 8 July 2026
- **REPORTED FACT.** FTC + AGs of Illinois, Arizona, Michigan, Minnesota, Wisconsin announced a proposed settlement (stipulated order) on **8 July 2026**, resolving the suit filed 15 January 2025 in N.D. Ill. (Western Div.).
  - FTC press release: `https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-states-secure-settlement-deere-company-advancing-farmers-right-repair` (8 Jul 2026)
  - FTC case timeline item: `https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/deere-company-ftc-v-timeline-item-2026-07-08`
  - Reuters via Yahoo: `https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/deere-settles-lawsuit-us-ftc-173711779.html`
  - Freshfields analysis: `https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/ftcs-john-deere-settlement-signals-scrutiny-of-aftermarket-repair-restrictions`
- **Terms:** for **10 years**, under FTC/state supervision, Deere must make available to every owner and independent repair provider, "on Fair and Reasonable Terms," repair resources (including applicable software capabilities) **equivalent to those provided to authorised Deere dealers**, subject to a Rollout Schedule. Most resources due on the order date (8 Jul 2026); select resources phased **1 Aug – 31 Dec 2026**.
- **Money:** **$1m** to cover the plaintiff states' legal costs. No civil penalty, no disgorgement.
- **Deere's framing (8 Jul 2026 release, VP Aftermarket & Customer Support Denver Caldwell):** the agreement "formalizes Deere's ongoing commitment" and "brings to a close the matter."
- **Forecast impact:** **negligible for Q3 FY2026 P&L** ($1m). Medium-term, it is a modest structural risk to the aftermarket parts-and-service margin pool that sits inside PPA and SAT — but the rollout only begins in Aug–Dec 2026, so essentially none of it lands in the May–Jul quarter. Deere had already disclosed at Q2 that it was "in discussions… with respect to a potential resolution" and was "unable to estimate the potential impact" (10-Q line 1884), so **no reserve was booked for it** — meaning no reserve release either.

## 5. Tariffs — the single most movable line item

**REPORTED FACT (Q2 10-Q, `filings/2026-05-28__de-us-20260528-q2-10q__1055932.md`, line 1880), verbatim on the allocation:**

> "…we prepared and filed a refund claim in the amount of $272, which has been accepted by the CBP. We recorded a recovery for this initial amount as we concluded the refund is probable and reasonably estimable. **The recovery was allocated 20%, 30%, and 50% to PPA, SAT, and CF, respectively, decreasing cost of sales.**"

Derived split (MY INFERENCE, simple arithmetic):

| Segment | Share | $m |
|---|---|---|
| Production & Precision Ag | 20% | **$54.4** |
| Small Ag & Turf | 30% | $81.6 |
| Construction & Forestry | 50% | $136.0 |
| **Total** | 100% | **$272.0** |

**Consequence for the PPA forecast.** Q2 FY2026 PPA reported operating profit **$706m on $4,503m net sales = 15.7% margin**. Strip the ~$54m one-off and the clean run-rate is **~$652m / 14.5%**. The Q2 PPA operating-profit walk (Q2 slides) shows Production Costs as a **−$77m** drag *even after* the tariff credit — i.e. underlying production cost inflation in PPA was roughly −$131m before the refund. Anyone extrapolating Q2's 15.7% into Q3 is extrapolating a one-off.

**Tariff run-rate (REPORTED FACT, Q2 call Q&A, corpus `call-transcripts/2026-05-21__de-us-20260521-call-qna__1042775.md`):** management said that netting IEEPA going away, Section 122 coming in, and Section 232 adjustments, *"our overall run rate for tariff expense really remains unchanged at about $1.2 billion for the full year."* Corroborated: `https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/deere-ieepa-tariff-refund-construction-demand-q2-2026-earnings/821705/` — $1.2bn ≈ **3 points of margin headwind** for FY26.

**MY INFERENCE — two-sided tariff risk in the Q3 print:**
- **Upside:** CBP's refund process advanced through phases 2 and 3 during Deere's Q3 (fiscal quarter ≈ 4 May – 2 Aug 2026). Deere explicitly described its $272m as *"this initial amount"* based on *"the eligibility parameters established by the CBP for the **initial phase**."* A **second refund tranche recognised in Q3 is a live possibility**, and 20% of any such tranche would flow to PPA operating profit. Additionally, the CIT's 7 May Section 122 ruling (stayed) creates a further contingent refund claim. This is the largest single upside wildcard in the Q3 PPA number.
- **Downside/neutral:** Section 122 → Section 301 swap on 24 Jul 2026 falls in the last ~9 days of the fiscal quarter, so its cost effect is mostly a Q4 issue. Management's $1.2bn full-year run rate already contemplated churn between tariff regimes.
- Confidence that a second tranche lands specifically in Q3: **low (~30–40%)**. I found no Deere disclosure either way. Treat as a fat right tail on PPA, not a base case.

This is the number the forecasting model must produce, and it is **not** the $10.87bn most headlines will print. Flagging loudly.

## 15. Directional take for the modellers

1. **Worldwide net sales & revenues (~$12.5bn consensus-implied).** Both the FY segment guides (PPA −5–10%, SAT +15%, C&F +20%) and the "back half higher than front half, abnormally back-half-weighted Waterloo shipments" comment support a y/y increase. C&F has beaten twice running (+29% in Q2). I see the risk here as **balanced to slightly upward** — the top line is the least contentious of the three targets.
2. **GAAP diluted EPS.** This is where the tension lives. Guidance arithmetic says ~$4.1–4.3; consensus says $4.85. Deere's beat record and the possibility of a second IEEPA tranche argue for the upper half. **My read: the model should sit below consensus, roughly $4.30–4.80, with a fat right tail.**
3. **PPA operating profit.** The most important and most mis-anchored number. Q2's $706m/15.7% contained ~$54m of non-recurring tariff refund. FY guidance of 11–13% margin on net sales down 5–10% (FY25 base $17,311m) implies FY26 PPA net sales of ~$15.6–16.4bn and FY26 PPA operating profit of ~$1.72–2.13bn. H1 FY26 PPA operating profit was $706m (Q2) plus Q1 — the team should complete that arithmetic to bound H2. Against a Q3 FY2025 comp of $580m / 13.6%, the combination of back-half-weighted Waterloo shipments (volume tailwind), +3% currency, ~+1% price, and the absence of the Q2 tariff credit points to a Q3 PPA operating profit **modestly above the $580m prior-year comp** but with margin nearer the low-to-mid teens than Q2's flattered 15.7%. Watch the right tail if CBP phase 2/3 refunds get recognised.
4. **Do not model:** a June-2026 guidance raise, a new buyback authorisation, a Q3 charge for the $99m class action, or a material Q3 charge for the FTC settlement. All four are either fabrications of low-quality aggregators or already accrued in prior periods.
