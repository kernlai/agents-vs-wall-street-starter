## 4. What exactly is adjusted out of "adjusted diluted EPS"

**REPORTED FACT** (Q1 FY2026 8-K, non-GAAP section,
`challenge/offline-data/home-depot/filings/2026-05-19__hd-us-20260519-q1-8k__1038584.md`):

> "The Company excludes the impact of amortization expense from acquired intangible assets … including
> the related tax effects, from adjusted diluted earnings per share."

**That is the ONLY adjustment.** No restructuring, no acquisition costs, no impairments in the current
definition. Mechanics:

| | Q1 FY2026 | Q1 FY2025 | Q2 FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| GAAP diluted EPS | $3.30 | $3.45 | $4.58 |
| + acquired intangible amortisation | $0.17 | $0.14 | — |
| − tax effect | $(0.04) | $(0.03) | — |
| **Adjusted diluted EPS** | **$3.43** | **$3.56** | **$4.68** |
| Pre-tax intangible amortisation | $171m (of which **$119m SRS**) | $139m ($87m SRS) | — |

The Q1 step-up in the add-back ($139m → $171m pre-tax; $0.11 → $0.13 net) is **GMS + Mingledorff's
purchase-accounting intangibles layering on top of SRS**. Company guidance: FY2026 adjusted EPS excludes
an expected **after-tax impact of ~$0.50** and adjusted operating margin excludes **~40bp**. $0.13 landed
in Q1, so ~$0.37 remains across Q2–Q4 → **~$0.12–0.13 of add-back in Q2 FY2026** (vs $0.10 in Q2 FY2025).
So GAAP-to-adjusted spread widens ~2–3c y/y — a small but real reason adjusted EPS grows faster than GAAP.

- **Comparable sales flat to +2.0%**

- Gross margin ~33.1%; operating margin 12.4–12.6%; **adjusted** operating margin 12.8–13.0%
- Tax rate ~24.3%; net interest expense ~$2.3bn; capex ~2.5% of sales
- Diluted EPS **flat to +4.0%** off $14.23 (GAAP) / **adjusted EPS flat to +4.0%** off **$14.69**

Sell-side FY2026 adjusted EPS consensus sits at **$14.69** (Barchart, 23 Jul 2026) — i.e. the street is
modelling the *bottom* of the guide (0% growth). Read-across: consensus is **not** assuming a Q2 beat
that would force a guidance raise.

**The single most important management quote for Q2 (Q1 FY2026 Q&A, 19 May 2026):**
> "We are not looking at a marked improvement in underlying demand. We are looking at a higher comp in
> the second half of the year, and that is solely driven by a return to normal storm activity."

So management has *already* told the street that H2, not Q2, carries the acceleration. A Q2 comp near
+0.6–1.0% is consistent with guidance being maintained; a Q2 comp materially below +0.5% would put the
flat-to-2% full-year comp guide under pressure.

## 7. Demand drivers — what to weigh

**Ticket vs transaction.** The split has been remarkably stable: ticket carrying the comp, transactions
negative. Q1 FY2026 was ticket **+2.2%** / transactions **−1.3%**. Zacks' consensus for Q2 implies the
same shape: **average ticket $92.48** (vs $90.01, **+2.7%**) and **retail transactions 439.99m** (vs
446.80m, **−1.5%**). That is the street modelling a *widening* of the ticket/transaction gap, consistent
with building-materials price inflation from tariffs. Sales per store $19.40m (vs $19.24m); store count
2,365 (vs 2,353).

**Pro vs DIY.** Q1: "pro posted positive comps and outperformed DIY." Pro strength in power, pipe &
fittings, water heaters, fasteners, paint. DIY strength was spring-seasonal (live goods, OPE, patio,
grills, storage) — which makes Q2, the largest selling quarter for those categories, the make-or-break
one. HD frames Pro as a $700bn TAM ($1.2trn including HVAC distribution post-Mingledorff's).

**Big-ticket / discretionary.** Q1 big-ticket comp transactions (>$1,000) turned **positive 0.8%** — the
first meaningful positive in a while — but "larger discretionary projects remain under pressure"
(permit-requiring remodels). Nine of 16 merchandising departments positive in Q1.

**Storms.** Q1 FY2026 carried a **−56bp storm headwind** (lapping Helene/Milton rebuild), and Billy
Bastek said "that'll dissipate throughout the balance of the year." So Q2 FY2026 loses ~half a point of
headwind versus Q1 — a mechanical **+~0.5pt tailwind to the reported comp sequentially**, before any
demand change. Separately, McPhail: "Q2 and Q3 of last year had some of the lowest recorded hail and
hurricane storms in history" — SRS laps that easy compare **starting in Q2 FY2026**.

**Housing turnover / mortgage rates (FRED, keyless CSV).**
- 30-yr fixed (MORTGAGE30US): 6.30% (30 Apr) → 6.48% (4 Jun) → 6.55% (16 Jul) → **6.69% (6 Aug)**,
  6.67% (13 Aug). Rates **rose through the whole of fiscal Q2** — an unambiguous negative for housing
  turnover and for the large-project pipeline.
- Existing home sales SAAR (EXHOSLUSM495S): May 4.19m, Jun 4.13m, **Jul 4.06m** — drifting down, near
  40-year lows on a per-household basis (Decker's framing on the Q1 call).
- Housing starts (HOUST): May 1.199m, Jun 1.427m — volatile, no trend improvement.
- Management on rates (Q1 Q&A): homeowners have "50% value pop" in home equity and healthy portfolios;
  "the main thing is just this uncertainty that's holding them back for taking on large projects."

**Category read-through — the one genuine positive surprise indicator.** Census advance retail sales,
building materials & garden equipment dealers (FRED **RSBMGESD**, seasonally adjusted, $m):
- HD fiscal Q1 window (Feb–Apr 2026): 124,971 vs 120,864 → **+3.40% y/y**
- HD fiscal Q2 window (May–Jul 2026): **126,995 vs 119,845 → +5.97% y/y**
i.e. the category **accelerated ~2.6pts** between HD's Q1 and Q2 windows, and the July print (42,583) was
the highest in the series. **CAVEAT (INFERENCE, medium-low confidence):** the HD-comp-to-category gap is
unstable quarter to quarter (Q1 FY2025 −0.7pt, Q2 FY2025 +2.35pt, Q1 FY2026 −2.8pt), the series is
nominal (so tariff-driven price inflation inflates it), and it covers competitors and channel mix HD does
not. Treat it as a modest tilt toward the upper half of the comp range, not as a signal to break far
from consensus.

**SRS / GMS.** Q1 FY2026: SRS delivered **$4.0bn** of sales, positive total and positive organic sales
growth, but **slightly negative comps** driven by low-single-digit-negative roofing comps — while taking
share by external measures. Cross-sell target across HD/SRS/GMS: **~$400m run-rate this year, doubling
next year**. GMS added $1.3bn to Q1 FY2026 net sales and drives the ~75bp gross-margin mix drag; it
anniversaries in **early September 2026**, i.e. **Q2 FY2026 is the last quarter with GMS fully
incremental to reported sales growth**. That is why FY total sales growth (2.5–4.5%) is well above comp
growth (0–2%) — and it is the mechanical reason net sales grows ~4–5% while comps grow <1%.

## 10. How to use this for the forecast (scoring logic)

We are scored **relative to consensus**, and a missing number scores 5.0. Concrete guidance:

1. **Net sales:** anchor **47,350 USDm**. The published spread (47,000–47,500) is ~1 scoring floor wide,
   so the choice inside it barely matters; what matters is not falling outside it. Two upward nudges are
   defensible and *articulable*: (a) GMS at seasonal peak likely tops its $1.3bn Q1 contribution;
   (b) Mingledorff's ~12 weeks at HVAC peak season is a source of upside most models under-count because
   the revenue was never disclosed. A number in **47,400–47,700** is the reasoned-deviation zone.
   Below 47,000 or above 48,000 is an unexplained gap and a risk, not an edge.
2. **Adjusted diluted EPS:** anchor **$4.72**. Consensus is exceptionally tight ($4.71–$4.73) and the
   floor is 0.5% of the result (~$0.024), so this metric is essentially a coin-flip around consensus —
   **do not deviate materially**. HD has printed within a couple of cents of consensus in each of the
   last several quarters ("in line with our expectations" is the standing management phrase). Remember to
   forecast **adjusted** (add back ~$0.12–0.13 of after-tax intangible amortisation to GAAP), not GAAP —
   a GAAP answer (~$4.59) would miss by ~13c, roughly 5.5 floors.
3. **Comparable sales:** anchor **+0.9 points**, and note the reasoned case for the upper half
   (**+1.0 to +1.5**): the −56bp Q1 storm headwind dissipates (+~0.5pt mechanically), FX was a +55bp
   tailwind in Q1 and the dollar has not reversed, May tracked like Feb/Mar per management, the
   building-materials category accelerated ~2.6pts in the Q2 window, and SRS laps a record-low hail/storm
   quarter. Against that: mortgage rates rose ~40bp through the quarter, existing home sales fell to
   4.06m, and management explicitly disclaimed any Q2 demand inflection. **Positive sign is the base
   case; a negative comp would require a sharp break from five consecutive quarters of flat-to-positive
   prints and is a low-probability tail.** Do not submit a decimal-shifted value: 0.9 means 0.9%.

## 11. Risks to this dossier

1. **No published headline comp consensus.** The +0.9 benchmark is triangulated (implied from revenue
   consensus + media framing + prior-quarter StreetAccount analogue), not quoted. If the organisers'
   "street miss" uses a StreetAccount/Visible Alpha number I could not see, my benchmark could be off by
   ~0.3–0.5pt — which on a 0.5pt floor is one whole scoring unit.
2. **Mingledorff's revenue is undisclosed.** My $150–300m Q2 contribution is an estimate from branch
   count; if it is a $1bn+/yr business at summer peak the sales number could beat consensus by $200m+.
3. **Zacks vs S&P Global disagree on revenue by $260m** (~0.55%, just over one floor). I report both.
4. **CEO medical leave (12 Aug)** is unmodelled by consensus and could change the *tone* of guidance
   (higher probability of "reaffirm" than "raise"), though it should not change the reported quarter.
5. **Census RSBMGESD acceleration may be an artefact** — the series steps up sharply from Jan 2026 and
   the HD-to-category gap has been unstable, so it is the weakest input here. Do not over-weight it.
6. **Tariff-driven price inflation** cuts both ways: it lifts average ticket (consensus already assumes
   +2.7%) but can suppress transactions and unit demand further than modelled.
7. Corpus metadata defects confirmed (period labels off by a year on several transcripts); all figures
   above were taken from document body text with an explicit dateline, never from front-matter.
