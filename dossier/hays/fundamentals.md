## 5. WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED IN FY2026 — the operating picture

### White-collar hiring market
Persistently weak all year with a clear **Temp-over-Perm** split and a **sequential improvement in the
YoY decline** through H2 (Q2 −10% LFL → Q3 −8% → Q4 −5%).
- **Perm** was the problem all year: FY26 quarterly LFL −13/−14/−12/−7%. Q4's −7% flattered by a soft
  prior-year comparator ("last year we actually had quite a tough Perm in Q4, particularly in May and
  June… following the tariff and all that sort of stuff that happened in April" — CFO, 10 Jul 2026).
  Perm volumes −10% in Q4; average Perm fee **+3%** (deliberate shift to higher-salary roles).
  Perm "softened slightly through the quarter" — job inflow in UK&I / Australia / Northern Europe
  **down ~5% vs Feb–Mar pre-Easter levels**.
- **Temp & Contracting** stable: Q4 LFL −3%, volumes −5%, price/mix/German-hours −1%. Germany average
  hours worked −5% YoY, "consistently where we were in the previous quarter". Mix shifted to
  **65% Temp & Contracting / 35% Perm** in Q4 26 (FY25: 62/38; H1 26: 64/36).
- **Bright spots:** Japan Temp & Contracting +76% in Q4 (Japan total +9%), Spain +33% T&C (total +23%,
  record quarter), Portugal +31%, Greater China +22%, Poland +6%, Germany Construction & Property +38%.
- **Weak spots:** France −17%, New Zealand −13%, Canada −8%, Germany Engineering −20%, Germany
  Accountancy & Finance −15%, public sector generally (ANZ public −14%, UK&I public −6%).

### 7.1 Net fees — **£904m** (ESTIMATE; high confidence)

**Method (validated on H1):** reported net fees = H1 26 actual + H2 26 built from H2 25 divisional

**Key risk (flagged, not adopted):** if Hays presents the six divested European countries as

business or geographical area of operations", and the Q4 statement says they "contributed c.£15m to
**reported group net fees in FY26**", i.e. they are inside the reported figure. Retain £904m.

### 7.2 Pre-exceptional operating profit — **£46.3m** (ESTIMATE; high confidence)

- Guidance: "at the top of the **£37.0–46.0m**" range ⇒ £46.0m floor for the estimate.
- CFO arithmetic: H2 26 "up about 30%" on H2 25's £20.1m ⇒ H2 26 ≈ £26.1m ⇒ FY26 ≈ **£46.2m**.
- Hays' habit of beating its own point guidance by 0.5–1.5% (FY25: c.£45m → £45.6m; H1 26: c.£20m →
  £20.1m) argues for a touch above £46.0m.
- Implied conversion rate 46.3/904 = **5.1%** (FY25 4.7%, H1 26 4.4%, H2 26 implied 5.8%) — consistent
  with £35m of H2 cost savings landing against a −5.3% H2 fee decline.

**Forecast £46.3m.** Range £45.8–46.8m. Consensus ≈£46.0m, so we sit +0.3m (+0.7%) above — a small,
articulable deviation (the beat-your-own-guidance pattern), well inside the 0.5%-of-result floor's
sensitivity.

**Tax rate is the main uncertainty.** At the FY25 results Hays guided FY26 ETR to **"c.38%"**; by H1 26
the actual charge was struck at **44.8%**, explicitly "based on the forecasted pre-exceptional effective
tax rate for the full year" — i.e. management's own FY26 forecast at February was 44.8%. The rate is
high because losses in some countries carry **no deferred-tax-asset recognition**. Since that
unrecognised-loss block is roughly fixed in £ terms while H2 profit came in above the February
expectation, the FY26 outturn ETR should land **slightly below 44.8%**. Modelling tax ≈ 30% ×
(PBT + unrecognised losses) with the loss block implied from the H1 charge gives an FY26 ETR of
**~43.3%**. I use **43.5%**.

## 8. RISKS TO THESE FORECASTS

1. **IFRS 5 restatement risk on net fees.** If the six divested countries (and/or any of the seven under
   review) are presented as discontinued operations, reported FY26 net fees could be ~£889m and FY25
   restated. Judged unlikely (see §7.1) but it is the single largest tail risk on the net-fee metric —
   a ~£15m, 1.7% error, ~3.3× the 0.5% floor. Consensus would be wrong in the same direction, which
   limits relative damage.
2. **Effective tax rate.** The 38% → 44.8% swing inside twelve months shows how volatile this line is.
   Country disposals, deferred-tax-asset write-ups/downs, or Pillar Two effects could push the FY26 ETR
   anywhere in 38–48%, moving pre-exceptional EPS between ~1.09p and ~1.27p. This is the widest
   uncertainty band of the three metrics.
3. **"Top of the range" could mean exactly £46.0m** and no more, especially if the new CEO wants a clean
   base for the 20 August strategy reset. A kitchen-sinking incoming CEO could also reclassify costs —
   though anything reclassified into *exceptionals* would flatter, not depress, the pre-exceptional line.
4. **Net finance charge.** The full pension buy-in (FY25) removed net pension finance income; the June
   property-consolidation programme changes the lease-liability base. £13.5m could be £12.5–14.5m,
   worth ±0.04p of EPS.
5. **Rounding of the disclosed quarterly growth rates** gives the net-fee estimate an irreducible
   ±£2.5m band even if the method is perfect.
6. **Share count / EPS denominator.** Weighted average shares of ~1,595m assumes the June-2026 buyback
   has minimal weighting. If Hays instead reports on a lower base, EPS rises marginally (~0.01p).
