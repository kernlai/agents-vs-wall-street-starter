## 6. Derived FY26 estimates — my independent build

### 6.1 Net fees — ESTIMATE **£903m** (consensus £902.4m)

H1 26 actual = £453.3m (FACT). H2 25 base = £476.4m (FACT).
H2 26 = £476.4m × blended Q3/Q4 actual growth of −7% and −4% ≈ £450m.
Sensitivity to rounding of the quarterly percentages (true values could be −7.4%/−4.4% or −6.5%/−3.5%) gives **H2 26 of £448–453m** and **FY26 of £901.5–906.5m**. The Q3/Q4 split of the FY25 base barely moves the answer.

**Bottom-up FY26 ≈ £903m.** Consensus £902.4m. Two independent corroborations: the CFO on the 10 July call described Hays as *"a business that does close to GBP 900 million"* in fees; and a regional build (Germany ≈ £288m, UK&I ≈ £176m, ANZ ≈ £114m, RoW ≈ £329m) sums to ~£907m.

Implied FY26: Temp & Contracting ≈ £580m (64%), Perm ≈ £325m (36%). Group actual decline ≈ −7.1%.

**Basis warning (the main tail risk on this metric):** FY26 reported net fees should **INCLUDE** the six disposed European countries. The Q4 statement states they *"contributed c.£15m to reported group net fees in FY26"* — an explicit confirmation that reported FY26 net fees are on the inclusive basis, with the exclusion applying only to LFL growth rates. If Hays instead applies IFRS 5 and restates them as discontinued operations, reported net fees drop to ~£888m; if the seven countries under review were also reclassified (£70m of fees), ~£818m. I judge both unlikely — these are small country operations, not a "separate major line of business or geographical area", the seven under review had no binding sale at 30 June, and the tight consensus range of £894–914m shows the sell-side is uniformly on the inclusive basis.

### 6.2 Pre-exceptional operating profit — ESTIMATE **£46.2m** (consensus £45.3m)

Two independent routes converge:
1. **"Top of the £37.0–46.0m range"** → ~£46.0m.
2. **CFO's own arithmetic:** *"this second half profit performance is up about 30% versus H2 last year."* H2 25 pre-exc operating profit = £20.1m → H2 26 = 20.1 × 1.30 = **£26.1m**. FY26 = H1 26 £20.1m + £26.1m = **£46.2m**.

Route 2 is the stronger evidence — the CFO was doing the sum out loud from near-final numbers ten days after the year end. **£46.2m is my point estimate**, with £46.0–46.5m the plausible band. Implied conversion rate 5.1% (FY25 4.7%).

**Why deviating from consensus is justified here:** the £45.3m mean is dragged down by an unrevised £40.0m low estimate; the top of the compiled range is £46.1m, which is where the company steered everyone. This is a well-reasoned gap, not an unexplained one. Risk if it lands exactly at £46.0m: our miss 0.2 vs street 0.7 — still a win.

### 6.3 Pre-exceptional basic EPS — ESTIMATE **1.15p** (consensus 1.13p)

```
Pre-exc operating profit            46.2
less net finance charge            (13.0)   [company guidance: "c.£13 million"]
= Pre-exc PBT                       33.2
less tax @ 45.0%                   (14.9)   [company guidance: "ETR in FY26 to be c.45%"; H1 26 actual 44.8%]

```
Cross-check on the consensus arithmetic: consensus EPS 1.13p on consensus operating profit £45.3m scales to 1.13 × 46.2/45.3 = **1.15p** at my operating profit. Both methods agree.

**Sensitivity (this is the softest of the three metrics):**

| FY26 ETR | Pre-exc PAT | EPS |
|---|---|---|
| 43% | £18.9m | 1.19p |
| 45% (guided) | £18.3m | 1.15p |
| 47% | £17.6m | 1.11p |
| 49% | £16.9m | 1.07p |

A ±1pt move in ETR is worth ±0.021p; ±£0.5m on finance charge is worth ±0.017p. **Directional risk is to the downside:** in FY25 the full-year pre-exceptional ETR (35.1%) landed 3.0pts ABOVE the H1 rate (32.1%), and FY26 H2 saw further losses in loss-making countries (France, the disposed/under-review portfolio) against which no deferred tax asset is recognised. If that pattern repeats, ETR lands nearer 47–48% and EPS nearer 1.10–1.11p. I hold 1.15p because the guidance was reiterated at half-year and the sell-side is anchored on it, but 1.13–1.15p is the honest band and a forecast below 1.13p is defensible.

## 7. Business context for FY26

### 7.1 White-collar hiring market through FY26
The "Great Hesitation" persisted all year: high job inflow (per-consultant activity near 2019 levels) but structurally depressed conversion to placement and materially longer time-to-hire. **Temp & Contracting was consistently the resilient half; Perm was the drag.** Perm net fees fell 13/14/12/7% LFL across the four quarters, driven by volume (Q3 −15%, Q4 −10%) partially offset by a consistent **+3% average Perm fee** as Hays deliberately targeted higher-salary roles. Temp volumes fell ~5% group-wide with price/mix/German-hours a further −1%.

Second-derivative improvement is real but modest: the CFO attributed part of Q4's better Perm to a soft prior-year comparator (*"we had a really sort of slow end to the last financial year in FY 2025, following the tariff and all that sort of stuff that happened in April"*). Temp & Contracting has felt sequentially stable *"since our second quarter"*, with the first decent New Year return-to-work in three years.

### 7.2 By region (Q4 26 LFL)
- **Germany** (32% of group net fees), −7%. Temp −7% (volumes −6%, hours/mix −1%); average hours worked stable through the quarter and in line with expectations — this had been the key negative surprise in Q2. Perm −12% but stable sequentially. Technology (36% of Germany) flat; Engineering −20%; Accountancy & Finance −15%; Construction & Property **+38%** on infrastructure/energy; public sector (18%) flat. Consultant headcount −6% QoQ, −16% YoY. Productivity +9%. Early, unconfirmed signs of auto-sector inbound recovery and possible fiscal-stimulus benefit (*"I probably need another quarter or two"*).
- **UK&I** (19%), −8%. Temp −5%, Perm −12% and softening through the quarter. Private sector (72%) −9%, public −6%. Technology flat, A&F −9%, C&P −2%, Office Support +1%. Headcount −4% QoQ, −16% YoY. Productivity +8%. Returned to profitability in H1 26.
- **ANZ** (13%), −2%. Temp −2%, Perm −1% but deteriorating through the quarter. Private (68%) +5%, public −14%. Victoria −12%, NSW −7%, ACT −22%; WA +17%, Qld +9% on resources/mining. NZ −13%. Headcount −6% QoQ, −8% YoY. H1 26 operating profit tripled.

Americas (23%) −2%, US −1% with improving momentum, Canada −8%. Asia (17%) **+8%**: Japan +9% (Temp +76%), Greater China +22%. Headcount −2% QoQ, −8% YoY.

### 7.3 Consultant headcount and productivity
- **Q4 26: consultant headcount −4% sequentially, −12% YoY** (excluding disposals/exits). Non-consultant headcount **−13% YoY**.
- **11th consecutive quarter of consultant net fee productivity growth, +8% YoY** in Q4 (Germany +9%, RoW +10%, UK&I +8%, ANZ +6%). FY25 was +5%.
- Group employees at 30 June 2026: **c.8,100 in 155 offices in 23 countries** (30 June 2025: c.9,500 in 207 offices in 31 countries) — a −15% headcount and −25% office reduction in one year.
- **Guidance: group consultant headcount to be broadly STABLE in Q1 27** — capacity now judged appropriate for current conditions. This is the swing factor for FY27 operating leverage.

### 7.4 Cost-reduction programme
- **c.£50m of annualised structural savings secured in FY26** — against a target of c.£45m pa by FY29, i.e. **delivered three years early**. Split: c.£15m in H1, **c.£35m in H2** (c.£20m in Q4 alone).
- **c.£115m pa of cumulative structural savings since the start of FY24** (c.£30m FY24 + c.£35m FY25 + c.£50m FY26).
- Drivers: Europe/Germany restructuring, shared-service and cash-cycle redesign in Finance, HR restructuring, global Technology function redesign, and a large property programme — **c.80 properties exited, consolidated or significantly downsized** in the final four months.
- Country disposals are **deliberately excluded** from the structural savings tally (*"we wouldn't see that as our actions to really reduce the underlying cost base, that is more mathematical"*), so the disposed six countries' ~£15m cost base and the seven under review (~£70m of fees, ~£70m of cost) sit on top.
- **A further cost programme and its restructuring charge will be announced at the 20 August results.**

### 7.5 Exceptional items — what to expect in the FY26 accounts
Hays classifies restructuring, impairments and transformation programme costs as exceptional. Flagged in the Q4 statement:
- **c.£40m exceptional restructuring charge** (from the acceleration of the cost programme in FY26);
- **c.£30m impairment of right-of-use assets** from the office consolidation;
- a **modest non-cash loss** on the disposal of the six European countries (net cash proceeds after costs c.£4m).

H1 26 already booked £8.8m. Whether the c.£40m is inclusive of that is ambiguous; **total FY26 exceptionals are likely c.£70–75m**, implying a **statutory operating loss of roughly £(25)–(29)m and a sharply negative statutory EPS**. Our metrics are all pre-exceptional, so this does not affect them — but do not confuse a headline statutory loss for a miss.

### 7.6 Portfolio reshaping
- 16 June 2026: completed sale of operations in **Czech Republic, Denmark, Hungary, Luxembourg, Romania and Sweden** to Meraki Capital, net cash proceeds c.£4m. Contributed **c.£15m of FY26 net fees and ~nil operating profit**.
- Exploring options for **Belgium, Brazil, Greater China, Malaysia, Netherlands, Singapore, UAE**. These 13 countries in aggregate: **c.£85m of FY26 net fees, broadly breakeven operating profit** (~£0–1m). At the 2018/19 peak they did c.£110–112m of fees and c.£13m of profit after central cost allocation.
- Earlier FY26 closures: Chile, Colombia, Thailand, Mexico.
- Hays will go forward with **16 core countries**; the CFO confirmed *"there'll be nothing else for review."*
